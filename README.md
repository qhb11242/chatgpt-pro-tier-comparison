# ChatGPT Pro 5x: What the $100 Tier Really Unlocks, How It Compares to Plus and Pro 20x, and How to Pay for It When Your Card Keeps Getting Declined

"Pro 5x" is not marketing shorthand invented by resellers. It is OpenAI's own label. In the usage tables published in OpenAI's Help Center, the columns read *Plus / Pro 5x / Pro 20x / Business Standard*, and on the Codex pricing page you'll see the same split. So if you went looking for "ChatGPT Pro 5x," you were reading OpenAI's documentation, not a third-party sales page.

The confusion starts immediately after that, though. There are now two things called ChatGPT Pro, they cost $100 and $200 a month, and both of them give you the same models. That last part surprises people.

## The two Pro tiers, in one sentence

Pro 5x costs **$100/month** and gives you roughly five times the Codex and Work usage allowance of Plus. Pro 20x costs **$200/month** and gives you twenty times. Everything else — the model lineup, the feature set — is the same on both.

The $100 tier was announced on 9 April 2026, slotting in between the $20 Plus plan and the existing $200 Pro plan, and it was aimed squarely at the same spend bracket as Anthropic's Claude Max. At launch OpenAI ran a promotion that doubled the advantage to 10x Codex usage through 31 May 2026. That promotion is over. If you find a blog post written in spring 2026 promising "10x," you're reading an expired offer.

Between the two Pro tiers you can switch at any time from Settings → My Plan, and OpenAI applies the billing adjustment automatically based on your billing cycle. Reaching a model's allowance does not cancel your subscription and does not mean your account was restricted — that model just goes temporarily unavailable until the reset time shown in your account.

Two mechanics that trip people up:

- **You are governed by two windows at once.** The five-hour window controls how much you can do in a burst. A separate weekly window caps total usage. You need headroom in both to keep going.
- **Local messages and cloud chats draw from the same pool.** Running Codex locally doesn't buy you extra quota elsewhere.

## What else $100 buys (and what it doesn't)

Beyond volume, the Pro tier unlocks things Plus doesn't get:

- **GPT-6 Pro in Chat**, powered by Astra, available on eligible Pro, Business and Enterprise plans. Its message limits sit outside the shared Work and Codex allowance. Plus gets Astra inside Work and Codex but not GPT-6 Pro in Chat.
- **Unlimited GPT-5.6 usage and file uploads**, faster unlimited image generation, maximum access to Deep Research and agent mode, and the highest memory and context ceilings.
- **Expanded project, task, and custom GPT quotas**, plus early access to features in research preview.

What it does **not** buy: Ultrafast, the low-latency Astra mode. OpenAI's documentation lists Ultrafast as available on the Pro $500 tier, with Plus, Pro $100, Pro $200 and Business excluded. Buying credits on those plans doesn't switch it on either.

And one thing worth internalizing before you upgrade: OpenAI support will not reset your usage limits. Not for Plus, not for Pro. If you hit a ceiling, you wait for the reset, use a saved reset, or keep working with another model.

## Who actually gets value out of ChatGPT Pro 5x

The honest filter is simple: **have you hit Plus limits recently?**

If you're a developer running long autonomous Codex sessions, you probably have. The pattern is familiar — you're three days into a billing cycle, the five-hour window pins at 100%, and you're waiting on resets with a queue of work. That's the exact gap the $100 tier was built to fill. Same for anyone leaning on agent mode and Deep Research all day rather than in bursts.

If you use ChatGPT for drafting, analysis, and the occasional script, five times your current allowance buys you nothing you'll notice. The jump from $20 to $100 is 400% in price, and the only thing scaling with it is volume. More capability isn't part of the deal — the model suite is identical between Pro 5x and Pro 20x.

A rough rule: if you can't point to a specific week where Plus stopped you mid-task, stay on Plus.

A separate thread suggests Pro customers were able to request refunds. So failures aren't always terminal — but they happen, and often in batches rather than one account at a time. The batch pattern is the tell: when something upstream changes, every account processed through that path is exposed at once, whatever the individual platform's intentions.

**Third-party risk scoring.** Scamadviser's automated check gives bewild.ai a low trust score, flagging a privacy-shielded owner identity, a recently registered domain, and shared hosting with sites that score poorly. This is an algorithm reading public records, not an investigation with findings — plenty of legitimate businesses hide WHOIS data. **The rulebook issue.** OpenAI's terms prohibit sharing account credentials, reselling access, and using ChatGPT to power third-party services. Any arrangement where someone else logs into your account to complete a purchase sits in awkward territory relative to that language. In practice, subscriptions bought through intermediaries do work for long stretches. But you are not in a channel OpenAI endorses, and the enforcement tools are theirs, not yours.

The practical takeaway isn't "never do this." It's that **you are paying for a month of access, not a guarantee**, and the price of the plan should be small enough that the loss is survivable. That argues for short terms on Pro specifically — 5x at $105.99 is already a real bill, and stacking three months of it at a platform whose renewal behaviour you haven't personally observed concentrates a lot of exposure.

## A short checklist before you pay

1. **Confirm you actually hit Plus limits.** Pull up Settings → Usage and look at your five-hour and weekly history. If there's always headroom, Pro 5x is $80/month you won't feel.
2. **Take a live screenshot of the checkout price.** The $105.99 figure comes from a mid-2026 snapshot. Rates and platform costs shift.

4. **Think about the session credential in advance.** You're granting login access to your account so someone else can complete a purchase and renew it. If you have sensitive material in that account, decide how you feel about that before, not after.
5. **Keep the order number, the account status screenshot, and the support conversation.** The documented cases that ended in refunds were the ones with a paper trail.
6. **Have an exit plan.** If your Pro access drops mid-month, what's your fallback for the work it was powering? If the answer is "there isn't one," don't put a critical deadline behind a third-party subscription.
